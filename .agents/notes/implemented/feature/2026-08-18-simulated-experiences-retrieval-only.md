# Agent Note: Simulated experiences — retrieval-only candidates with a reality-monitoring exit

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-08-18-simulated-experiences-retrieval-only.zh.md)

## Problem

The cold loop is correctly deferring on the real store: the labeled-validation gate (see [cold-loop acceptance](2026-08-18-cold-loop-acceptance-first-build-and-deferral.md)) refuses to cluster when too few experiences carry real labels. But deferral is not the same as progress. In cold start, the experience store has too few verified samples to cluster, retrieve, or predict from — and waiting for more is the passive answer. Humans do not wait: when direct testing is cheap and safe they run many trials; when it is not, they reason by analogy from similar situations or simulate mentally. The cognitive pipeline has no analogue of the third path — model-based simulation — and its existing exploration channel (the scratchpad) only reuses an action when the model happens to predict the same action again.

## Decision

**Simulated experiences enter the store as retrieval-only candidates with a `simulated` flag.** They are generated by the LLM route (given a situation and a proposed action, produce a predicted outcome and a provisional utility), stored with `simulated: true`, and become visible to retrieval, prediction reference, and the orchestrator's injection — but never to cold-loop clustering. A simulated experience shapes no cluster centroid and enters no taxonomy rule; only real, verified feedback can unlock clustering eligibility. This is the cognitive-science split the design is modeled on.

**The layering mirrors human memory.** Reality monitoring (Johnson & Raye) distinguishes remembered from imagined content — the `simulated` flag is the machine equivalent. Consolidation only for the real — mentally simulated options do not enter the sleep-replay consolidation channel; the cold loop is that channel and simulated experiences do not enter it. Simulation provides hypothesis space, not accepted knowledge — simulated samples feed retrieval and prediction reference but are not treated as stable strategy until verified.

**A simulated experience leaves the store through three channels, all driven by one principle — evidence replacement.** Its lifecycle is entirely driven by real evidence: evidence sufficient upgrades it, evidence covering it down-weights it toward expiry, evidence contradicting it rolls it back. Day counts, feedback counts, and confidence scores are proxies for this principle on different signal channels; none of them is the rule itself.

1. **Verified by real feedback** — `report_outcome` on a prediction bound to the simulated experience folds the feedback's evidence weight into `evidenceScore`. A single decisive weight at/above `simulationFastTrackThreshold` fast-tracks to `provisional`; cumulative evidence at/above `simulationPermanentThreshold` upgrades to `verified`. A contradictory feedback at provisional rolls back to `unverified` with the score reset.
2. **Superseded by real evidence (primary)** — verified experiences in the same situation down-weight and eventually expire the simulation, mirroring that mental rehearsal leaves no trace once the real thing is done.
3. **Validation timeout (fallback only)** — an unverified simulation with no feedback and no supersession within `simulationTtlMs` (default 30 days) expires and is removed from retrieval.

**Clustering eligibility is the single gate, driven by cumulative evidence, not hit count.** The upgrade condition is `Σ feedback_i 的置信度权重 w_i ≥ simulationPermanentThreshold`; each feedback contributes a weight from how decisive the quality is. Three consistent medium-confidence feedbacks can cross the threshold; one high-confidence decisive feedback can too. The fast track grants provisional, not permanent, status — the calibration caveat is real, so a later contradiction rolls back.

**The shipped surface:** `Experience` gains `simulated: boolean`, `verification: 'unverified' | 'provisional' | 'verified'`, and `evidenceScore: number` (ordinary `remember_experience` writes are `verified` from birth). A new `simulate_experience` tool and `ctx.cognitivePipeline.simulate()` generate simulated experiences via the LLM route. Retrieval (`retrieveTopK`, orchestrator `retrieve()`, inject priming) includes simulated samples; cold-loop `sample()` excludes unverified ones. `report_outcome` drives the evidence-replacement state machine for bound simulated experiences, alongside the existing quality-backfill into the utility label.

## Alternatives considered

**Simulated samples participate in clustering from the start.** Rejected: it would let unverified inference shape cluster centroids and taxonomy rules — the exact pollution the cold loop's labeled-validation gate exists to prevent, reintroduced through the back door.

**Keep simulated samples in a separate pool, merge only when verified.** Rejected: a separate pool duplicates the store's persistence, retrieval, and lifecycle machinery; the `simulated` flag on the shared store with clustering exclusion achieves the same separation with one authority (the "one label source" rule from the [feedback-backfill note](2026-08-18-feedback-folds-result-quality-into-experience-label.md)).

**Rely on verification and density alone, no fallback timeout.** Rejected: real feedback may never arrive for a given simulation, and density may never reach the supersession threshold for an isolated situation; without a backstop the store accumulates stale unverified inference. The long fallback TTL guards that corner.

## Consequences

A simulated experience is retrievable and injectable but never appears in a cluster, taxonomy rule, or cold-loop sample; only verified or provisional samples may shape clusters. Real feedback upgrades it via the evidence rule — cumulative evidence crosses the threshold → permanent `verified`; a single decisive feedback → provisional `verified` that rolls back on contradiction — after which it participates in clustering. Verified density in the same situation down-weights and expires the simulation; the fallback TTL guards the never-verified case. The cold-start path is now active: when real labels are scarce, the model can generate reasoned projections that advise prediction without polluting the taxonomy, and the evidence-replacement lifecycle keeps the store free of stale inference. Package tests, typecheck, lint, and doc gates are green; the `simulate_experience` tool surface is covered by loader and pipeline tests, the state machine by store tests, and cold-loop exclusion by a dedicated rebuild test.

## Risks

- **Simulation quality drift**: LLM-generated outcomes may be systematically optimistic. Mitigated by the verified gate (only real feedback upgrades) and by simulated samples being retrieval candidates only — they advise, they do not assert.
- **Retrieval noise**: simulated candidates may surface before verified ones when labels are scarce. Accepted — that is the intended cold-start behavior; verified samples out-rank them as labels accumulate.
- **Verification never arrives**: the fallback TTL expires the simulated experience; the store does not accumulate stale inference.
- **Density signal sensitivity**: the "verified density" threshold that supersedes a simulation is itself a proxy; tuned via the same evidence-strength framing rather than a magic constant.
